## Shipped

- `uv run python -m aletheia_resolve run` (`py/resolve/`): reads the temporary raw donors (`calaccess:raw:*`) read-only from the CAL-ACCESS DuckDB, a parquet extract or Postgres, and writes `members.parquet` (raw entity → resolved entity, match score, confidence), `likely_links.parquet` (pairs labeled "likely the same donor"), `pairs.parquet` and `summary.json` to `data/work/resolve/`.
- Normalization: uppercase, punctuation, titles, suffixes (JR, SR, MD...), legal forms (INC, LLC, CORP...); people split into first, middle and last name, with the generational suffix kept separately. Joint gifts ("MICHAEL AND ELISA LEVY") are not merged.
- Blocking: people on last name + ZIP3, organizations on normalized name. Separate Splink models for people (first name, middle initial, last name, employer, occupation, city, ZIP5; Jaro-Winkler and term frequencies) and organizations (name, city, ZIP5), committed as `py/resolve/models/*.json`.
- Thresholds in `py/resolve/thresholds.json`: ≥ 0.95 merged (high), 0.80–0.95 "likely the same donor" (linked, never merged), < 0.80 separate.
- Hard rules on top of the model: committees, candidates, measures, lobbying firms and filers are never merged; people and organizations are never compared; people with clearly different first names, JR vs SR, or MR vs MRS/MS are never merged; merges are constrained so one entity never holds two different full first names, middle initials, generational suffixes or MR/MRS titles.
- `draft-labels`: 200 candidate pairs in `data/manual/labels.csv` (140 people, 60 organizations) across six strata, each with both records and the state's filing PDF. `report [--apply]`: weighted precision and recall per threshold into `py/resolve/REPORT.md`, and raises the threshold until precision ≥ 0.98.
- `load [--dry-run]`: writes the last run into Postgres in one transaction, additively and without a schema change (resolved key, score and confidence in `entity.attrs`, one `method = 'splink'` alias per merged record, `attrs.likely_same_donor` links). Reruns replace the previous resolution. Approved by the project owner as an exception to the agents-never-write rule.
- All 200 pairs labeled by agent:resolve (labeler column says so), to be checked by a human before launch.
- 18 tests on 25 real records (Yeager Sr./Jr., the Sobrato family, a joint gift, an LLC, a raw committee), 2 of them against a throwaway Postgres (`TEST_DATABASE_URL`).

## Broke or learned

- Letting Splink's EM estimate name weights gave m(first name exact) = 0.05: EM blocked on last name + ZIP3 found "same household or same office" instead of "same person". Name m probabilities are now fixed priors; EM estimates employer, occupation, city and ZIP.
- Even then, relatives and coworkers scored high: 244,419 person pairs at or above 0.99 have clearly different first names (final run) (two correctional officers named LOPEZ in West Sacramento, a married couple at one company). The first-name rule drops them.
- Stripping suffixes merged Jacques S. Yeager Sr. and Jacques S. Yeager Jr. of Riverside, directly and through records with no suffix. Fixed with the JR/SR rule plus constrained clustering.
- Term-frequency weights come from the input, so scores depend on the dataset: a 25-row test sample makes a rare company name look common. Same input gives the same result; new data can shift scores.
- Two retrains differed in the 16th digit (DuckDB parallel float sums) and in Splink's random `linker_uid`; saved models are now rounded to 10 significant digits without the uid.
- The person mistakes left at high scores are mostly relatives with near-identical first names where only one record has a title (Jane / Mr. Jan Smit; Marion Ashley / Mrs. Marion Ashley), very common names (two James Smiths with State of California jobs in ZIP 95811), and a first initial matched to the wrong full name (C. / Cindy Anderson). The MR/MRS rule only helps when both records carry a title.
- Organizations look too cautious: all 15 labeled "separate" pairs (same normalized name, different ZIP, e.g. two "Political Data" spellings in Norwalk) are the same organization.
- The report originally scored labels with the probabilities frozen at draft time. It now re-scores labeled pairs against the current run and post-stratifies, so it stays valid after model changes.
- Neon (`aletheia-dev`) has no CAL-ACCESS rows yet (0 raw entities, 0 money edges, no load running), so `load` could not run there. All numbers come from the local DuckDB build of the same export.
